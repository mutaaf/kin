01

Pairing you can't see.

No QR code, no camera, no microphone, no permission prompt. Slide one finger off the edge of your screen and onto the next. Each phone reports half a gesture: out at my right edge at t, in at my left edge at t′. The relay links the two halves that line up in time and face each other.

  • Calibrates the seam. The same swipe tells each screen which edges touch and how far the screens are offset vertically.
  • Refuses to guess. If two pairs swipe at the same instant, both are asked to try again.
  • Opt-in by gesture. The relay connects only when someone makes the swipe.
Live diagram

02

Two renders, one illusion.

Nothing is transported across the glass. Each screen draws its own creature, and both evaluate the same hop curve in one shared pixel space, so the motion is continuous across the gap. The seam is crossed near the top of the jump, where a little latency or a bezel reads as air.

  • Clock-synced start. NTP-style offsets put both screens on the same frame, a few hundred milliseconds ahead.
  • Same physical size. Linked iPhones and iPads ease to the same millimetres per unit, so a KIN never pops in size at the seam.
The app's own curve

03

Never lost. Never duplicated.

A crossing is two small state machines with a lease: the owner keeps the creature, the host only borrows it. Every step can be retried, and duplicates are answered instead of redone. This box runs the real machines through a hostile network right now, in your browser.

  • 20% of messages dropped, 15% duplicated, up to 400 ms of jitter, plus random crashes on both sides, recalls and "send home".
  • The rule: once the network settles, the KIN is home, or hosted by exactly one screen. Never both. Never neither.

04

Your sky, not a skybox.

The diorama follows your real time of day and sun angle, the temperature, the wind and the rain or snow outside. Rain sends a KIN under the mushroom, cold makes it shiver, wind moves the grass and its ears. Location starts coarse, from your timezone, and needs no prompt. Precise sky is opt-in and rounded to about 11 km.

05

A brain that shows its work.

A KIN picks what to do a few times a second by scoring its options: needs, temperament, weather, memories, a little seeded variation. No model runs every frame, and every choice comes with its reason. Move the sliders and watch it change its mind.

    An AI director, with your own key.

    Ask what your KIN is thinking.     07

    One seed, the same KIN everywhere.

    A KIN's look and temperament are a pure function of its seed, so a crossing sends a number, a name and a few traits, never a mesh. The web, iPhone and iPad apps are one codebase and render the same creature. It's private by default: no account, no real names, nothing to scan, and no data collected.
